# The Lion Roars: Judgment Begins with the Nations

Amos was a shepherd from Tekoa, a small town south of Jerusalem. He was not trained as a prophet. He was not part of the religious establishment. God took him from following the flock and sent him to prophesy against Israel.

The lion has roared. Who will not fear?

## The Lord roars from Zion

> "The Lord roars from Zion and utters his voice from Jerusalem; the pastures of the shepherds mourn, and the top of Carmel withers."

This is not gentle instruction. This is the roar of a lion about to strike. God speaks from Zion, and creation itself trembles. The lush pastures wither. Even Carmel, known for its fertility, dries up.

**When God speaks in judgment, nothing is untouched.**

## A pattern of indictment

Amos begins with the nations surrounding Israel. Each oracle follows the same pattern: "For three transgressions, and for four, I will not revoke the punishment."

This is not a precise count. It means their sins have reached the point of no return. The cup is full. Judgment is certain.

Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, Ammon. One by one, the enemies of Israel are condemned.

**Israel would have loved this. Finally, their enemies face God's wrath.**

## The sins of the nations

Each nation is condemned for specific atrocities:

Damascus threshed Gilead with sledges of iron.
Gaza carried whole populations into exile to Edom.
Tyre delivered captives to Edom and broke the covenant of brotherhood.
Edom pursued his brother with the sword and kept his anger forever.
Ammon ripped open pregnant women to expand their territory.

These are crimes against humanity. Violence. Slavery. Betrayal. Cruelty. God sees them all.

**The nations will answer to the God of Israel for how they treat human beings.**

## No one escapes

The point of this opening is setup. Amos circles Israel like a lion stalking prey. He condemns the distant nations first, then the closer ones.

The Israelites listening would nod in agreement. Yes, those nations deserve judgment. Yes, God should punish them.

But the net is tightening. The prophet is not finished.

**Judgment on others is easy to accept. Judgment on ourselves is another matter.**

## God's justice is universal

Amos reveals that God holds all nations accountable, not just Israel. The Syrians, Philistines, Phoenicians, Edomites, and Ammonites do not worship Yahweh. They do not have the Law. Yet they are judged for moral atrocities.

This is because God's moral law is written on the human heart. Nations that practice cruelty and violence will answer for it.

**The Lord is not a tribal deity. He is the Judge of all the earth.**

## Final reflection

Amos 1 sets the stage. The lion is roaring. Judgment is coming. The nations fall one by one under divine condemnation.

But Israel should not rejoice too quickly. The same God who judges the nations will judge His own people with even greater severity.

Those who know more are held to a higher standard.

## Closing prayer

Father, You are the Judge of all nations. You see every act of violence, every cruelty, every injustice. Teach us to fear You rightly and to live justly before You. May we not celebrate judgment on others while ignoring our own sin. In Jesus' name, amen.
