# The Lion Roars: The Net Tightens on Israel

The indictments continue. Moab is condemned. Then suddenly, Judah. And finally, the true target: Israel.

The lion has been circling. Now it strikes.

## Moab's crime

> "For three transgressions of Moab, and for four, I will not revoke the punishment, because he burned to lime the bones of the king of Edom."

Moab desecrated the dead. Even enemies deserve dignity in death. To burn bones to lime is to erase someone completely, to deny them even the honor of burial.

**God cares about human dignity, even for the dead, even for enemies.**

## Judah falls under judgment

> "For three transgressions of Judah, and for four, I will not revoke the punishment, because they have rejected the law of the Lord, and have not kept his statutes."

Now the shock begins. Judah, the southern kingdom, David's line, the keeper of the temple, they too face judgment.

Their sin is different from the nations. They have rejected God's law. They have the Torah and have abandoned it. They followed the same lies their fathers followed.

**Privilege increases accountability. Judah had the law and ignored it.**

## Israel: the primary target

> "For three transgressions of Israel, and for four, I will not revoke the punishment."

This is where Amos has been heading all along. Israel, the northern kingdom, the prosperous nation that thinks itself blessed, they are the chief offender.

The indictment is devastating:

They sell the righteous for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals.
They trample the head of the poor into the dust.
A man and his father go to the same girl, profaning God's holy name.
They lay themselves down beside every altar on garments taken in pledge.
They drink wine bought with fines in the house of their god.

**Economic exploitation. Sexual immorality. Religious perversion. Israel has it all.**

## God's gracious history

Amos reminds Israel of what God did for them:

He destroyed the Amorites before them, tall as cedars and strong as oaks.
He brought them up out of Egypt.
He led them forty years in the wilderness.
He raised up prophets and Nazirites among them.

This is the tragedy. God gave them everything. He saved them, led them, provided for them, spoke to them.

And they silenced the prophets and made the Nazirites drink wine.

**Ingratitude compounds guilt. To sin against grace is worse than sinning in ignorance.**

## The inescapable judgment

> "Behold, I will press you down in your place, as a cart presses down when it is full of sheaves."

God Himself will bring the crushing weight of judgment. There will be no escape.

The swift will not flee.
The strong will not retain their strength.
The mighty will not save their lives.
The archer will not stand.
The fast runner will not escape.
The horseman will not save his life.
Even the bravest warrior will flee naked.

**When God moves in judgment, no human resource avails.**

## Final reflection

Amos 2 completes the indictment. What began with distant nations ends with Israel. The very people who thought themselves safe because of their covenant relationship are the most guilty.

They had the law. They had the prophets. They had the history of redemption. And they squandered it all.

This is the danger of religious privilege: assuming that proximity to God guarantees safety while living in defiance of His commands.

## Closing prayer

Father, we have received so much. Do not let us presume upon Your grace. Search our hearts for the sins we tolerate while judging others. Let us hear the roar of the lion and tremble. In Jesus' name, amen.
