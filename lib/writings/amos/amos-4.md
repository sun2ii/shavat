# Justice and Privilege: Yet You Did Not Return

God has sent warning after warning. Famine. Drought. Blight. Locusts. Plague. Sword. Each disaster was meant to turn Israel back to God.

They did not return.

## The cows of Bashan

> "Hear this word, you cows of Bashan, who are on the mountain of Samaria, who oppress the poor, who crush the needy, who say to your husbands, 'Bring, that we may drink!'"

Bashan was famous for its well-fed cattle. Amos addresses the wealthy women of Samaria as pampered cows. They lounge in luxury while the poor are crushed.

They demand that their husbands bring them more, never considering where the wealth comes from or who suffers to provide it.

**Luxury built on injustice is an abomination to God.**

God swears by His holiness: they will be taken away with hooks and fishhooks, led out through breaches in the wall like cattle to slaughter.

## The mockery of false worship

> "Come to Bethel, and transgress; to Gilgal, and multiply transgression; bring your sacrifices every morning, your tithes every three days."

This is biting sarcasm. God mocks their religious devotion. They love their rituals. They bring offerings, tithes, freewill offerings. They publicize their generosity.

But their worship is transgression. Their religion is rebellion. They love the performance of piety while practicing injustice.

**Religion without righteousness is an offense to God.**

## Five warnings, no repentance

Amos recounts how God tried to wake Israel up:

**Famine:** "I gave you cleanness of teeth in all your cities, and lack of bread in all your places."

**Drought:** "I withheld the rain from you... yet you did not return to me."

**Blight and locusts:** "I struck you with blight and mildew; your gardens and vineyards... the locust devoured."

**Plague:** "I sent among you a pestilence after the manner of Egypt."

**Military defeat:** "I killed your young men with the sword and carried away your horses."

Five times God struck. Five times the refrain: "Yet you did not return to me, declares the Lord."

**Discipline that does not produce repentance only hardens the heart.**

## Sodom's fate nearly theirs

> "I overthrew some of you, as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and you were as a brand plucked out of the burning; yet you did not return to me."

Some cities were destroyed like Sodom. Some were snatched from the fire at the last moment. Yet even this did not produce repentance.

Israel has become worse than Sodom. At least Sodom never had the warnings Israel received.

## Prepare to meet your God

> "Therefore thus I will do to you, O Israel; because I will do this to you, prepare to meet your God, O Israel!"

The warnings are over. Now comes the reckoning. Prepare to meet your God.

This is not an invitation to worship. This is a summons to judgment. Israel will stand before the God they have ignored, the God whose discipline they dismissed, the God whose prophets they silenced.

> "For behold, he who forms the mountains and creates the wind, and declares to man what is his thought, who makes the morning darkness, and treads on the heights of the earth, the Lord, the God of hosts, is his name!"

This is the God they face. Creator of mountains and wind. Knower of thoughts. Master of light and darkness. Lord of the heights.

**No one prepares for this meeting and survives unchanged.**

## Final reflection

Amos 4 reveals God's patience and Israel's stubbornness. Warning after warning went unheeded. Disaster after disaster failed to produce repentance.

There comes a point when warnings cease and judgment arrives. Israel has reached that point.

The question for every generation is the same: Will we hear the warnings and return, or will we harden our hearts until it is too late?

## Closing prayer

Father, do not let us ignore Your discipline. When You send warnings, soften our hearts to receive them. Grant us repentance before judgment, return before it is too late. In Jesus' name, amen.
