# A Marriage of Sorrow: Bought Back

Hosea 3 is brief and devastating. The prophet is commanded to love again the woman who has betrayed him. She must be purchased back. The marriage becomes gospel.

## Love her again

> "And the Lord said to me, 'Go again, love a woman who is loved by another man and is an adulteress, even as the Lord loves the children of Israel, though they turn to other gods and love cakes of raisins.'"

The command is explicit: love her again. Not a new wife. The same unfaithful woman. She has gone to another man. She remains an adulteress.

And yet: love her.

Why? Because this is how the Lord loves Israel. They turn to other gods. They love the raisin cakes offered in pagan worship. And still God loves them.

**God's love is not a response to our faithfulness. It persists despite our unfaithfulness.**

## The price of redemption

> "So I bought her for fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley."

Gomer has sunk so low she must be purchased, perhaps from slavery, perhaps from a cult prostitution context. The price is not impressive: fifteen shekels and some barley, about the price of a slave.

Hosea pays for what was already his. He redeems the one who abandoned him.

**This is the shape of redemption: paying the price for those who do not deserve it.**

## A season of discipline

After purchasing her, Hosea imposes conditions:

> "And I said to her, 'You must dwell as mine for many days. You shall not play the whore, or belong to another man; so will I also be to you.'"

Gomer is not immediately restored to full marital intimacy. There is a period of dwelling together without full relationship. She must not return to prostitution. She must not belong to another.

And Hosea will be to her, faithful, present, waiting.

This corresponds to Israel's experience:

> "For the children of Israel shall dwell many days without king or prince, without sacrifice or pillar, without ephod or household gods."

Israel will be stripped of both legitimate worship (king, prince, sacrifice, ephod) and illegitimate worship (pillar, household gods). A time of discipline. A time without either the true God or false gods.

**Discipline removes the substitutes so that only God remains.**

## Afterward they will return

> "Afterward the children of Israel shall return and seek the Lord their God, and David their king, and they shall come in fear to the Lord and to his goodness in the latter days."

The discipline has purpose. Afterward, return. They will seek the Lord and the Davidic king. They will come trembling to God's goodness.

"In the latter days" points forward. This is not just the return from Assyrian exile. This is eschatological hope. The final restoration. The ultimate seeking of God and His anointed king.

**Discipline leads to seeking. Seeking leads to finding. Finding leads to trembling joy before God's goodness.**

## The gospel in Hosea

This chapter is compressed gospel:

The bride is unfaithful.
The husband loves her still.
He pays the price to redeem her.
She is brought into a season of purification.
Ultimately, full restoration comes.

This is what God does for His people. This is what Christ does for His church. We are Gomer, unfaithful and enslaved. God is Hosea, loving and redeeming.

**The price is paid. The bride is bought. The discipline is purposeful. The restoration is certain.**

## Final reflection

Hosea 3 condenses the entire prophetic message into a single act. Love an adulteress. Buy her back. Discipline her. Wait for restoration.

This is not sentimental love. It is costly love. It is love that pays the price. It is love that disciplines without abandoning. It is love that will not let go.

This is how God loves. This is how Christ loves His church. And this is how we are called to love.

## Closing prayer

Father, thank You for buying us back when we had sold ourselves to idols. Thank You for the discipline that stripped away our false loves. Thank You for the restoration that awaits. Teach us to love as You love. In Jesus' name, amen.
