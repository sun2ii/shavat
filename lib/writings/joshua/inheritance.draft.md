# Intro
The battles have largely ended. Joshua no longer records armies marching or kings falling, but boundaries, families, inheritances, and names. What appears to be a record of land is actually a record of stewardship.

God's promise to Abraham has become specific. The inheritance is given, yet every tribe must still faithfully possess, cultivate, and protect what the Lord has entrusted to them.

# Memorial Stones
Joshua slows the story after victory. Boundaries are measured, promises are remembered, and inheritances are distributed because gifts from God are meant to be stewarded, not merely celebrated.

The following stones preserve what Joshua 13–17 teaches about receiving, possessing, and faithfully carrying forward the promises of God.

# Chapter 13 — Stewardship
## Story
Joshua had grown old, yet much of the land still remained unconquered. Rather than waiting for every battle to end, the Lord commanded Joshua to begin distributing the inheritance among the tribes.
## Tension
Can God’s people faithfully steward a promise that has not yet been completely possessed?
## Revelation
God assigns the inheritance before it is fully occupied. His promises invite faithful participation rather than passive waiting. Receiving the inheritance is the beginning of responsibility, not the end of faith.
## Stones
- God gives before His people fully possess.
- Stewardship begins where conquest ends.
- An assigned inheritance still requires faithful possession.
- Receiving is not the end of obedience.
- There is always more land to possess.
- God's promises move forward even when the work remains unfinished.
## Quote
You are now very old, and there are still very large areas of land to be taken over.

# Chapter 14 — Wholeheartedness
## Story
After waiting forty-five years, Caleb reminded Joshua of the promise made through Moses and asked for Hebron—the mountain still occupied by the Anakites.
## Tension
Will decades of waiting weaken faith or deepen it?
## Revelation
Caleb remembered God's promise long after others had forgotten it. Wholehearted devotion endured through waiting, and courage chose the hardest inheritance because it trusted the God who had spoken.
## Stones
- Wholehearted faith outlives long seasons of waiting.
- God's promises are remembered across generations.
- Faith asks for the mountain rather than the valley.
- Waiting is preparation, not abandonment.
- The Lord preserves those who follow Him wholeheartedly.
- The greatest inheritance often requires the greatest courage.
## Quote
Now give me this hill country that the Lord promised me.

# Chapter 15 — Stewarding the Gift
## Story
Judah received its inheritance. Caleb drove out the Anakites, secured Hebron, and blessed the next generation, while Judah left the Jebusites living in Jerusalem.
## Tension
What happens after God's people receive what He has promised?
## Revelation
The inheritance reveals character. Caleb finished what had been entrusted to him, but Judah settled for incomplete obedience. The greatest danger was no longer failing to receive the gift, but failing to faithfully steward it.
## Stones
- Every inheritance becomes a stewardship.
- Finish what God entrusts to you.
- Faithfulness must continue after victory.
- A faithful legacy blesses the next generation.
- Small compromises become future strongholds.
- God's gifts must be cultivated, not merely possessed.
## Quote
Judah could not dislodge the Jebusites.

# Chapter 16 — Compromise
## Story
Ephraim received its inheritance but allowed the Canaanites to remain in Gezer, making them forced labor instead of driving them out.
## Tension
Will God's people obey completely when compromise appears profitable?
## Revelation
Partial obedience often disguises itself as practical wisdom. What seems useful today becomes tomorrow's influence. The inheritance is weakened whenever obedience is negotiated.
## Stones
- Partial obedience is still disobedience.
- Convenience often competes with faithfulness.
- What remains eventually influences.
- Small compromises quietly compound.
- Strength should produce obedience, not comfort.
- Faithfulness refuses profitable compromise.
## Quote
They did not dislodge the Canaanites living in Gezer.

# Chapter 17 — Responsibility
## Story
The daughters of Zelophehad faithfully received the inheritance promised through Moses, while Ephraim and Manasseh complained that their portion was too small because the Canaanites possessed iron chariots.
## Tension
Will God's people steward what they have before asking for more?
## Revelation
Joshua refused to reward complaint. Instead, he called the tribes to clear the forests and drive out the Canaanites. God's blessings become responsibilities before they become reasons for receiving more.
## Stones
- Remember what God has promised.
- Steward what you have before asking for more.
- Blessing increases responsibility.
- Excuses never overcome God's promises.
- The hardest work often guards the greatest inheritance.
- Though the enemy is strong, the Lord is stronger.
## Quote
Though the Canaanites have chariots fitted with iron... you can drive them out.

# Synthesis
eyebrow: The Movement
heading: How the Promise Became an Inheritance
## Opening
Joshua 13–17 is not merely an account of land being divided. It is an account of a people learning how to faithfully steward what God had already given them.

Each chapter answers a different question.
## Steps
- How should God’s people respond when the promise has been given but not fully possessed? → Stewardship
- How should God’s people respond after long seasons of waiting? → Wholeheartedness
- How should God’s people respond once the inheritance has been received? → Stewarding the Gift
- How should God’s people respond when compromise appears easier than obedience? → Compromise
- How should God’s people respond when they believe they need more? → Responsibility
## Closing
Receiving the inheritance was never the finish line. God called His people to faithfully possess what remained unfinished, cultivate what had been entrusted, and guard against the compromises that quietly erode faithfulness.

God was not only giving Israel the land. He was forming a people capable of faithfully stewarding His promises.

# Canon — The Inheritance Canon
- Steward what God has already entrusted to you.
- Wholehearted faith endures through long seasons of waiting.
- Finish what God has assigned.
- Refuse the compromises that weaken tomorrow.
- Faithfully cultivate every gift God gives.
