# Intro
Joshua is buried and the land is not taken. The book opens by asking the right question — who shall go up first for us against the Canaanites? — and then answers it with a catalogue of what each tribe left undone.

Israel does not lose the land here. It stops taking it. And within a single generation the people who watched the Jordan stand still are gathered to their fathers, leaving children who were never told.

# Memorial Stones
Joshua set twelve stones at Gilgal so that when children asked what they meant, their fathers would have something to say. Judges opens with those children. No one asks.

These stones are set against that silence — what Israel kept, what it left standing in the land, and how quickly a deliverance goes unremembered.

# Chapter 1 — Compromise
## Story
Judah went up first and took Bezek, and Adoni-Bezek, who had gathered seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off to glean under his table, was given the same and said so. Caleb gave Achsah the upper and lower springs when she asked, because a field in the Negeb without water is no inheritance. Then the chapter turns and does not turn back: Judah could not drive out those of the plain, for they had chariots of iron; Benjamin left the Jebusites in Jerusalem; Manasseh left Beth-shean and Taanach and Megiddo; Ephraim left Gezer; Asher lived among the Canaanites rather than dispossess them; and the Amorites pressed Dan back into the hill country.
## Tension
Will Israel finish taking the land it has already been given?
## Revelation
The chapter records no defeat. It records a stopping. Every tribe named had strength enough to begin and not will enough to finish, and when Israel later grew strong it put the Canaanites to forced labor instead of driving them out — a bargain that has the shape of victory and none of its substance. What is left undone does not stay undone. It stays.
## Stones
- Judah went up first because the Lord was asked first.
- Adoni-Bezek measured his own judgment by the seventy kings under his table.
- Achsah asked for springs, because land without water is a promise you cannot live on.
- Chariots of iron are named as the reason; the God who dried the Jordan is not.
- Forced labor is what a people call a conquest they did not finish.
- The Canaanites persisted in the land because Israel permitted it, not because Israel was beaten.
- Dan was pressed back into the hills, for ground not taken becomes ground lost.
## Quote
They did not drive them out.

# Chapter 2 — Forgetting
## Story
The angel of the Lord came up from Gilgal to Bochim and said that the covenant would not be broken, but that the nations left in the land would be thorns in Israel’s sides and their gods a snare. The people lifted their voices and wept, and named the place Weeping. Then Joshua died at a hundred and ten and was buried at Timnath-heres, and all that generation was gathered to its fathers, and another generation rose after them that did not know the Lord or the work He had done for Israel.
## Tension
What survives of a deliverance once the people who saw it are buried?
## Revelation
Nothing survives on its own. The generation that crossed the Jordan did not fail to remember; the generation after it was never told. Out of that silence comes the shape of the whole book — they served the Baals, the Lord gave them into the hand of plunderers, He raised up judges who saved them, and when the judge died they turned back and were worse than their fathers. The covenant held. The memory did not.
## Stones
- The angel names the covenant unbroken and the consequence certain in the same breath.
- What was left in the land became thorns and snares — not a punishment added, but the compromise grown up.
- A place can be named for its weeping and nothing after it change.
- Joshua was buried at a hundred and ten; what he carried was not buried with him, it was simply not handed on.
- A generation that did not know the Lord had parents who did.
- The Lord raised up judges, and Israel would not listen even to them.
- Each turning ended lower than it began, because relief under pressure is not return.
## Quote
There arose another generation who did not know the Lord.

# Synthesis
eyebrow: The Movement
heading: How the Conquest Came Undone
## Opening
Judges 1–2 is not the account of a war going badly. It is the account of a people who stopped fighting one, and then forgot why it had ever been fought.

Each chapter answers a different question.
## Steps
- What becomes of a promise that is only half possessed? → Compromise
- What is lost when the witnesses are buried? → Forgetting
## Closing
The two chapters are one motion. What Israel left standing in the first is what its children served in the second, exactly as the angel at Bochim said it would be.

Nothing that follows is a surprise. The judges, the cycles, the long descent that ends with every man doing what is right in his own eyes — all of it is already planted here, in a conquest set down before it was finished and a memory nobody carried forward.

# Canon — The Unfinished Conquest Canon
- Ask the Lord who shall go up first, before you go up.
- Finish driving out what you were given to drive out.
- Take no tribute where you were told to take the land.
- Tell the next generation the works they did not see.
- Weep at Bochim, then leave Bochim changed.
